🚰 **Using Data Science to Predict Water Pipe Failures Before They Happen**

Water utilities manage thousands of kilometres of pipelines, and one of the biggest challenges is knowing **which pipes are most likely to fail and where maintenance should be prioritized**.

I recently developed a **Pipe Failure Prediction Machine Learning project** to explore how historical water infrastructure data can be used to identify patterns associated with pipe failures and support proactive maintenance decisions.

🔎 **What does the model do?**

The model learns from historical pipe and infrastructure characteristics to estimate the likelihood of a pipe failure.

Instead of waiting for a pipe to burst and then responding to the problem, a predictive approach can help utilities identify **higher-risk assets in advance** and investigate them before they become major failures.

🌍 **What could this look like in the real world?**

A water utility could use a prediction system to:

• Identify pipelines that require closer inspection
• Prioritize preventive maintenance
• Support pipe replacement and rehabilitation planning
• Reduce unexpected pipe bursts
• Minimize service interruptions to customers
• Improve maintenance scheduling
• Allocate limited infrastructure budgets more effectively
• Support long-term asset management decisions

For example, if a utility has thousands of kilometres of pipelines but limited funds for rehabilitation, a predictive model can help provide an additional **data-driven layer for deciding which assets deserve closer attention**.

💧 **Why is this important for water infrastructure?**

Pipe failures are not only an engineering problem.

A failed pipeline can result in:

➡️ Water losses
➡️ Interrupted water supply
➡️ Emergency repair costs
➡️ Damage to surrounding infrastructure
➡️ Reduced reliability of the water network
➡️ Increased operational pressure on utility teams

For water utilities, infrastructure projects and asset managers, moving from **reactive maintenance to more proactive, risk-informed maintenance** can potentially improve how infrastructure is managed over its lifecycle.
